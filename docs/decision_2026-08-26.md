### The flooring defect (fixed at source)

`L(mu)` is exact in real arithmetic, but is evaluated as a sum of many floats.
About 1e-13 of accumulated error can place the computed value just below an
integer; `floor()` then removes a whole profit unit and the bound is no longer
valid. The trigger is systematic, not rare: as the subgradient converges,
`L(mu)` approaches the integral optimum **from above**, so it sits adjacent to
an integer exactly when convergence occurs — i.e. on precisely the instances a
certificate is most likely to close. Observed on **5 of 9** converged instances.

Fixed in all three sites with a 1e-9 tolerance. Appendix A's ad-hoc safeguard
and its named-instance anomaly have been removed and replaced with the
principled treatment, cited to Neumaier & Shcherbina (2004). The corrected bound
for that instance reproduces the 0.068 % the appendix had derived independently
from the BPC optimum — the diagnosis confirmed from two unrelated directions.

## 1. The best paper we can submit

Two options. They differ in ambition and in risk, not in correctness.

### Option A — refinement as a certification pass (conservative)

Present the method as published, plus an offline certificate-refinement stage.

* No rerun of anything. Incumbents, tables, figures all stand.
* Headline becomes 42.4 % → 67.6 % proven optimal, mean gap halved.
* Fits inside the existing 1-hour per-instance envelope: the slowest solve is
  40.8 min, so no instance approaches the cap even with refinement added.
* Zero tuned parameters on the primal side; nothing selected post hoc.

### Option B — Option A plus the runtime/quality frontier

Adds the anytime characterisation: a trade-off curve traced by replaying the
recorded phase trajectories, with the published configuration marked on it.

* Also requires no rerun — the frontier is read from recorded state, not
  simulated. Truncating a trajectory returns what the solver actually held.
* Supports the runtime story: **mean −31 %, max −49 %** at the aggressive rung.
* Costs: a new figure and section, and it obliges the paper to report that
  92.8 % of runtime follows the last incumbent improvement.

**Recommendation: A, with B as a bounded subsection rather than a second
contribution.** B's material is genuinely interesting but it is *supporting*
evidence for why refinement works. Promoted to a headline it invites
"your search is 93 % unproductive — why is this the design?", and the honest
answer is future work. Kept as support, it reads as command of the method.

### What the contribution claim should be

Not a methodological discovery. Lagrangian relaxation and ALNS are standard,
and the budget-allocation observation is close to folklore once stated — we
should not claim novelty for it. The defensible claim is:

> First certified method for SRPS, advancing the benchmark from 42 % to 68 %
> proven optimal with rigorous, independently re-derived per-instance
> guarantees.

That is checkable, and it needs no novelty argument propping it up.

### Addendum 2 — sweep and frontier complete

**Refinement iteration-cap sensitivity** (Appendix B). Measured on the 60
loosest instances, the only ones where the cap can bind:

| cap | mean gap | closed | median time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0.5061% | 4/60 | 35 s |
| **3000 (used)** | **0.4666%** | **10/60** | 96 s |
| 5000 | 0.4643% | 10/60 | 154 s |

3000 sits at the knee: halving costs six closures and 0.040 pp, doubling buys
0.002 pp and nothing else for 60% more time. The parameter is demonstrably not
finely tuned.
